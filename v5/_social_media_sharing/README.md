## Recommended outreach strategy

I would avoid sending the same announcement to all 100 accounts simultaneously. The stronger approach is to start with five or ten carefully selected organizations, use a specific problem they already work on, and ask for an independent evaluation.

The message should establish three things: what Pathway Forge currently does, what it demonstrably does not do, and what precise collaboration you are requesting.

Hello,

I've developed **Pathway Forge**, an open-source, human-governed framework for exploring and comparing multidisciplinary solutions to complex public-interest problems.

It combines structured problem definition, evidence requirements, solution-pathway generation, feasibility screening, adversarial review, and measurable pilot planning.

I'm looking for qualified organizations willing to independently evaluate its methodology or test it against a narrowly defined, real-world challenge in their existing work.

Would someone on your innovation, research, or program implementation team be appropriate to contact for a technical review?

I can share the demonstration, source code, documentation, and known limitations.

Foster + Navi\
Planetary Restoration Archive

One final distinction matters: we can confidently tell these organizations that a new open-source solution-development tool exists. We should not tell them that verified solutions to the Russia–Ukraine conflict already exist because the OS generated candidate pathways. That conclusion would require diplomacy-specific validation, operational feasibility, and involvement by legitimate stakeholders.

My recommendation is to seek five independent technical reviews and one narrowly scoped pilot before pursuing the major diplomatic institutions.

That would give the outreach a much stronger foundation: actual evidence that the system helps organizations produce better decisions, rather than a claim they are being asked to accept on trust.
