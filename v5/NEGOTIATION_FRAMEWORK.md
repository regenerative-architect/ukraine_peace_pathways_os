# Negotiation feasibility and implementation framework

This framework addresses four known disconnects between generative open-source peace designs and real-world war termination: **authority**, **incentives**, **credible commitments**, and **implementation**. It does not provide tactical military advice or assert private motives.

## 1. Separate peace-making functions

**Humanitarian interventions**: focused relief, prisoner exchanges, family tracing, cross-line passages, protection of critical civilian infrastructure. Humanitarian law applies independent of bargaining. A neutral intermediary needs party agreement and safe access.

**Sectoral restraints**: tightly defined reciprocal restrictions such as civilian energy infrastructure restraint. Need exact prohibitions, bilateral acceptance, synchronized effect, evidence standards, dispute review, command-level communication and breach response.

**Armistice mechanisms**: larger cessation of hostilities, separation, independent observation, security and incident investigation. A signature does not itself supply monitor access or enforcement.

**Political settlement**: territorial and security positions, legal sovereignty, rights, justice, alliance policy, sanctions and recovery. Requires authorized sovereign approvals and third-party decisions. De facto control must not be confused with legal recognition.

**Regenerative recovery**: restoration of water, power, housing, food security, habitats, jobs and accountable procurement. Can be valuable while war persists, but cannot substitute for sovereign political decisions or lawful security.

## 2. Strategic bargaining test

Ask with dated sources: What does each principal publicly demand? What are their own plausible alternatives if negotiations fail? How might continuation costs and external assistance change those alternatives? Can both parties understand a proposal's reciprocal incentive? Could either enjoy benefits while violating terms? Which domestic audiences and institutions can block implementation? Does enforcement make re-escalation less attractive without coercing protected people?

These questions are about *political-strategic feasibility*, not tactical analysis. Do not calculate attack plans, expose troop locations, or optimize force employment.

## 3. Veto-point dependency chain

`Idea → legally competent owner → public positions and legal constraints → actual scope discussed → explicit principal-party acceptance → third-party mandates → domestic approval/funding → monitoring access → incident adjudication → operational delivery → independent outcomes.`

A failure in any mandatory link yields **unresolved** or **blocked**, not an averaged score. No number of humanitarian partners can replace the principal parties' assent to a ceasefire. Conversely, lack of comprehensive settlement does not automatically bar lawful, narrowly consented humanitarian assistance.

## 4. Verification grammar

For each clause, provide:

- **Object:** what exact action is prohibited, permitted or required, and what is not covered.
- **Jurisdiction:** who can accept/commit this clause and on what legal authority.
- **Clock:** public effective date, review period and expiration.
- **Observer:** genuinely mandated independent body, funding, safety and confidentiality obligations.
- **Evidence:** independent baseline, observable aggregate indicator, corroboration, chain of custody where lawful, acknowledged gaps.
- **Dispute:** incident allegation intake, due process, adversarial views, correction and appeal.
- **Response:** lawful consequences for violations/denied access, escalation limit, who decides.
- **Civilian impact:** meaningful safety and protection assessment with privacy-preserving public reporting.
- **Durability:** financing, maintenance, ownership transfer and recurring review.

Historical OSCE monitoring provides lessons but the 2014–2022 OSCE SMM cannot be treated as an existing deployment in October 2026.

## 5. External impact audit

A professional panel should review completeness, bias, conflict-sensitivity, source quality, legal competence, affected-community consultation, safeguards, budget realism and implementation. Successful handoff requires real acknowledgement; successful negotiation requires real agreement; successful protection requires independent evaluation against harm and service indicators with limitations noted.

Institutional endorsement, outcome attribution and lasting peace are *not* generated by this framework. If a plan is blocked, the legitimate next research move may be to narrow the scope, find competent lawful owners, preserve independent humanitarian channels, and wait for independently verified changes.
