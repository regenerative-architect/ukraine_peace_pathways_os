# Two worked feasibility examples — no authorization implied

This document illustrates the value of the v2 redesign: proposals are useful only to the extent that the required institutions, agreements and safety conditions can independently be established.

## Case A: Oleshky humanitarian relief access (16 October 2026)

**Issue class:** Narrow, protected civilian access. Not a settlement, not a generalized ceasefire.  
**Current public signal:** Reuters reported a Kremlin statement supporting a Vatican-backed initiative to facilitate aid to people in Russian-controlled Oleshky. The public statement does **not** alone confirm a ceasefire, a full route/safety agreement, third-party deployment or actual aid delivered. The ICRC has explained that it cannot act without the parties' agreement and appropriate guarantees where it would endanger civilians.

**Diplomatic problem:** Public political support is not the same as the authority to secure both sides, guarantee humanitarian passage or actually deliver safe assistance. The site must not automatically assign the Vatican or ICRC roles or claim either organization accepted.

**Actor/consent map (research hypotheses):**

| Role | Required verification before any public operational claim |
|---|---|
| Ukrainian competent authorities | Whether the proposed civilian access scope, cooperation and protection measures have their explicit relevant acceptance |
| Russian competent authorities | Whether the reported public support yields actual lawful and verifiable safe access and cooperation |
| Humanitarian implementer | Whether a legitimate organization agrees to implement within its neutral mandate and resources |
| Civilian safeguarding / independent observers | Whether impartial observation is permitted and feasible without compromising sensitive persons |

**No-go triggers:** Lack of safe access; competing instructions; civilian coercion; disclosure of locations or identities; parties disagreeing on the exact scope; official approval incorrectly inferred from the press.  
**Legitimate next research step:** Determine whether a recognized intermediary has published a verified agreed scope and mandate. Verify independently. Without it, draft only a public, aggregate humanitarian-needs briefing. Do not publicize a corridor, move people or make direct operational representations on this tool's authority.  
**Testable future outcome, if independently authorized:** Aggregated aid received and civilians assisted, independently documented while safeguarding people and source limitations; no user-entered proxy for real deliveries.

## Case B: Reciprocal energy-infrastructure restraint (October 2026)

**Issue class:** Sectoral interstate restraint. Not a generalized armistice or durable security settlement.  
**Current public signal:** Reuters on 11 October reported Ukraine awaited full details of a U.S.-announced energy ceasefire proposal, while attacks continued. Further reporting on 12 October included rejection of a separate proposed 60-day pause. Different announcements and scopes should not be collapsed into “the sides agreed.”

**Necessary decision points:**

1. Public text or authenticated instrument with **identical** definitions and synchronized acceptance by authorized Ukraine and Russia representatives. Neither a U.S. announcement nor a social post alone meets this condition.
2. Compliance instructions delivered through appropriate national command authorities; discuss at strategic agreement level, not tactical force information.
3. Defined objective conduct; protected civilian-object obligations apply regardless of the proposed bargain.
4. Credible independent reporting and safe procedures for contested alleged violations. A new OSCE mission cannot be assumed; its previous SMM closed in March 2022.
5. Lawful and credible non-compliance responses, mutually understood off-ramp and safeguards against civilian energy deprivation and retaliatory escalation.

**No-go triggers:** Unilateral acceptance, unverified attribution, monitoring obstruction, politically unsustainable terms, unsafe disclosure of infrastructure vulnerabilities or contractual authority absent.  
**Legitimate next research step:** Collect the latest identical accepted terms from directly accountable institutions before assigning a monitor, classifying compliance or claiming an operational pause. A first useful deliverable is a *contradiction-and-unknowns memo*, not a numerical pathway rating.

Sources: Reuters 11 October 2026 https://www.reuters.com/world/europe/ukraine-awaits-details-ceasefire-proposals-us-russia-maintains-attacks-zelenskiy-2026-10-11/ ; OSCE closure https://www.osce.org/special-monitoring-mission-to-ukraine ; UN Charter https://www.un.org/en/about-us/un-charter/full-text .

## What this demonstrates

These examples are **not breakthrough deals**. They are disciplined *filters* that distinguish a meaningful, narrow civilian-protection entry point from a plan that accidentally ignores the decision-making machinery of the conflict. The application is a place to record and audit those filters; actual diplomatic, humanitarian and security authority must exist outside the software.
