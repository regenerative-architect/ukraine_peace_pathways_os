# Cross-jurisdiction legislative compatibility research guide

Edition: **v4.3 · 16 October 2026**

## Intended use and legal boundaries

This module helps researchers compare proposed civilian protection, demilitarization and reconstruction **research questions** across **28 national/supranational/international authorities and reference systems**. Its grid covers 16 mechanisms × 28 jurisdictions = **448 pairings**. Only **115 source-mapped provisional research flags** have been authored. **333 are deliberately unassessed.** This is not legal counsel, automated sanctions screening, certification of current law, proof of authorization or a negotiating mandate.

Use the term *jurisdiction* here to include reference institutions (such as IAEA, ICRC and OSCE) whose documents are not generally binding national legislation. A standards publication does not give that institution powers outside its legal mandate. Official-source URLs are provided for research; most publisher pages are **not** locally cached and may change after this snapshot.

## How categories work

| Flag | Research significance | Not a conclusion of |
|---|---|---|
| Constitutional/sovereignty conflict | A specific identified legal or sovereignty obstacle to the proposal as framed | Binding court ruling in an individual case |
| Sanctions/restrictions screening | Specific official sanctions regimes that must be evaluated against exact entities, benefits, transaction and jurisdictional nexus | The transaction is prohibited or licensed without further review |
| Specific authority / mandate required | Competent authorization, approved mandate, documented consent or accreditation must exist | The authority has agreed to participate |
| Framework or policy review | Official legal/standards source identified; applicability not fully adjudicated | A general source applies to this proposal |
| Not assessed | No case-specific source is mapped | Permission, prohibition, or non-applicability |

**Cumulative obligations:** Any actor with multiple jurisdictions of legal nexus may need to comply with all of them. For EU members, direct EU regulation is displayed alongside national law and enforcement. A UK/US/Canadian approval does not override EU restrictions and vice versa. Humanitarian protections never become optional bargaining rewards.

## Research method

1. Describe exact proposed conduct and persons involved without adding confidential or identifying information to a public output.
2. Select one intervention family and the primary actor's jurisdiction. Inspect the reference links in the independently scrolling right panel.
3. Include all affected regulators and jurisdictions (where relevant, donor, banking, transport, custodian, receiving government, facility and treaty obligations). Select up to five at once for comparison.
4. Confirm which sources are binding current consolidated legislation, government guidance, technical standard or mere portal. Note article number, in-force date, amendment and other laws likely to apply.
5. Treat sanction-list changes, licensing/waivers, confiscation powers, arms/export rules, sovereign immunity and statutory procurement as **unresolved** until checked with competent legal practitioners.
6. Note each approval/document that must exist; explicitly stop on missing consent, unlawful recipient, incompatible constitutional requirement, rights violation or unknown legal status.
7. Export an individual review note to obtain professional review, or transfer it into Pathway Forge for a structured research plan. These files are draft memoranda, not government requests or approvals.

## Key official source entrypoints (reviewed October 16, 2026)

- Ukraine: [Constitution, Verkhovna Rada](https://zakon.rada.gov.ua/laws/show/en/254%D0%BA/96-%D0%B2%D1%80); territorial alterations invoke constitutional provisions including Article 73.
- EU: [Regulation 833/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A32014R0833), [Regulation 269/2014](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2014/269/oj/eng) and [Commission sanctions guidance](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/eu-and-world/sanctions-restrictive-measures/overview-sanctions-and-related-resources_en).
- US: [OFAC Russian Harmful Foreign Activities sanctions and general licenses](https://ofac.treasury.gov/sanctions-programs-and-country-information/russian-harmful-foreign-activities-sanctions).
- UK: [Russia sanctions statutory guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/russia-sanctions-guidance/russia-sanctions-guidance) and [OFSI licensing](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/licences-that-allow-activity-prohibited-by-financial-sanctions).
- Canada: [Russia sanctions regulation](https://laws.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2014-58/index.html) and [permit conditions](https://www.international.gc.ca/world-monde/international_relations-relations_internationales/sanctions/certificates_permits-certificats_permis.aspx?lang=eng).
- UN/IMAS: [IMAS 07.11 on land release](https://www.mineactionstandards.org/standards/07-11/) is professional guidance, not a military or land-control mandate.

## Explicit limits

The grid makes no legal predictions from unreviewed countries. It does not fetch official law or monitor sanctions amendments. Many countries are represented only by a legal portal; these entries are research pointers rather than legal applicability findings. It is not a substitute for transaction-level diligence and cannot authorize diplomatic conduct, security monitoring, territorial changes, handling of explosive materials or regulated finance.
