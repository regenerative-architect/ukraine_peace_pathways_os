# Evidence-to-institutional-review protocol

**Not an instruction to contact parties or represent an official process.** This is a reproducible *research* sequence to convert a concept into a narrowly scoped, falsifiable brief. Assumes the research analyst has no diplomatic mandate.

## Research chain: seven passes

1. **Frame an actionable issue.** Choose one of 32 issue dossiers. Write a single observable civilian protection objective and explicit exclusions. Distinguish a humanitarian activity, reciprocal sectoral restraint, monitored armistice and final political settlement.
2. **Locate legal competence.** Identify all institutions that must approve the arrangement. Ask what legal instrument gives them authority, what ratification is needed, and whose consent may not be delegated. If answer unknown, keep gate unknown.
3. **Source all public positions and contradictions.** For each affected principal, retrieve a recent *primary* source and a separate reputable secondary account. Record publication and statement dates; avoid substituting journalists' analysis for a party's agreed text. Cross-check opposing claims.
4. **Adversarial bargaining model.** Document nonagreement alternatives, strategic incentives, domestic political constraints, sanction and alliance decision rights, and potential asymmetric benefits. Do not invent private motives, operational force dispositions or probabilities.
5. **Implementation and verification chain.** Require terms, signatories, effective time, safe authorized access, budget, independent baseline, incident reports, dispute procedure, obstruction response, civilian safeguards, and lawful stop mechanism. Lessons from past monitoring apply; assumed mission names do not create mandates.
6. **Humanitarian and rights check.** Evaluate non-coercion, return rights, vulnerable groups, trauma-sensitive practice, access conditions, aid independence, gender and disability inclusion, and protection against publication of private records.
7. **Outside review and honest handoff.** Export a memo labeled unverified; circulate only through legitimate professional channels with informed consent and never present a generated draft as agreed or institutionally endorsed. Log critiques, changes and unresolved blockers locally.

## Reusable multi-pass research prompts

These can be pasted into a research assistant using actual web research and citations. The analyst must examine originals before using any output in decisions.

**A. Authority and veto points**
> For the specified Russia–Ukraine civilian-protection measure, identify each actor with genuine legal approval, implementation, funding or verification authority. Cite dated official documents, explain domestic and multilateral consent requirements, and identify where authority is unknown. Do not mistake capability for authorization or consent.

**B. Public-position confrontation**
> Retrieve the latest dated primary statements of authorized Ukrainian and Russian bodies that specifically bear on this narrow proposal, plus relevant guarantor and EU/NATO positions. Distinguish public demands, actual signed text, secondary reports, irreconcilable requirements and areas explicitly left open. Cite direct URLs and timestamps; omit uncorroborated private intent.

**C. Strategic feasibility without tactical detail**
> Analyze publicly documented high-level bargaining incentives, war continuation alternatives, external security/economic support, domestic political constraints and asymmetric defection risks. Provide competing interpretations and specific evidence that would falsify each. No tactical intelligence, frontline coordinates, targeting or advice to evade defenses.

**D. Red-team inversion scan**
> Assume the draft is exploited through ambiguous wording, unilateral acceptance, forged authority, denied monitor access, selective incident statistics, unfunded guarantees, propaganda, or humanitarian coercion. Identify harm pathways, affected civilians, independent checks, lawful stop rules and safeguards. Do not produce operational exploitation instructions.

**E. Rights-preserving incremental agreement**
> Determine whether a narrower humanitarian outcome—voluntary family reunification, verified detainee exchange, infrastructure repair access, medical passage—has clearer legitimate implementers than a comprehensive settlement. Explain why it still requires consent, safety and neutral implementation; identify relevant organizations' actual remits without naming them as volunteers.

**F. Implementation evaluation**
> Draft a publicly understandable monitoring charter: operational scope at policy level, authority, baseline, funding, access, anonymized reports, evidence standards, dispute review, enforcement limits, expiration and independent outcomes evaluation. Explicitly list what cannot be verified from public sources.

**G. Institutional handoff**
> Turn the analysis into a concise briefing memo with [aim; dated positions; actual decision holders; legal risks; consent prerequisites; low-regret next research move; monitoring/finance; red-team objections; falsification criteria; consulted sources]. Mark every inferred claim and make no claim of mandate, contact or acceptance.

## Disconfirming questions to answer every time

- What public document actually shows **both** parties agree to the same exact restriction?
- Would the scheme change bargaining power without protecting people? What evidence supports either claim?
- What actors or legislation can block a supposedly credible economic or security incentive?
- What happens if a monitor cannot access the site or a report is contradicted?
- What humanitarian obligations exist regardless of a negotiated exchange?
- How do affected civilians consent to any voluntary movement without coercion or data exposure?
- Who would operate and fund the arrangement for a year, and who audits it?
- What observation would cause us to abandon or substantially revise this proposal?

## Quality-control levels (not game scores)

- **Research hypothesis**: proposal and source list exist; authority or consent unknown.
- **Reviewable draft**: source claims, legal concerns, veto points, disputes, implementation and failure cases are explicit; no external endorsement implied.
- **Institutionally considered**: independent proof an authorized organization acknowledged the specific proposal; this is *external evidence*, not an app state.
- **Agreed and operational**: separate signed bilateral/multilateral instruments and independently verified implementation; cannot be certified by this application.

A document with missing facts stays a hypothesis. Progress is measured by real-world independent evidence, not checkmarks.
